Competitor Analysis for Uday Sangle’s Campaign
– Sinnar Constituency (2026)
Introduction
The Sinnar assembly constituency in Nashik district has traditionally seen contests between heavy‐
weight local politicians. Uday Sangle, a former youth activist who turned politician, contested the 2024
assembly election as the Nationalist Congress Party (Sharad Pawar faction) candidate and came
second to the NCP (Ajit Pawar) candidate Manikrao Kokate. As the campaign prepares for the next
electoral challenge, understanding the main competitors – their profiles, strengths, weaknesses and
recent controversies – is crucial. This report synthesises available information up to June 2026 with
citations from credible sources.
Political Landscape of Sinnar
Historical results. The Sinnar seat was held by Rajabhau Waje of Shiv Sena in 2014 before 
Manikrao Kokate of the Nationalist Congress Party (NCP) won in 2019 and retained the seat in 
2024 . According to the 2024 election results, Kokate received 138,565 votes (57.36 %), while 
Uday Sangle obtained 97,681 votes (40.43 %); the victory margin was 40,884 votes. The
results highlight both the incumbent’s strength and the challenger’s significant voter base.
Key communities. Sinnar’s electorate includes Maratha, OBC (Mali, Vanjari, Dhangar and other
groups), Scheduled Castes, Muslims and other minorities. Farmers and co‑operative society
members also play an influential role due to the constituency’s agrarian economy.
Dominant issues. Voters often raise concerns about irrigation, crop prices, unemployment,
transport infrastructure and corruption in local bodies. High‑profile criminal cases or political
controversies can influence voter sentiment.
Main Competitors
1 – Manikrao Kokate (NCP – Ajit Pawar faction)
Aspect Evidence & details Analysis
Experience &
positions
Kokate has been elected MLA for Sinnar five times
since the 1990s. He served as a cabinet minister in
the Maharashtra government and has switched
parties multiple times (Indian National Congress,
NCP , Shiv Sena, Congress, BJP and back to NCP).
After winning two terms with NCP , he joined
Shiv Sena when denied a ticket; he returned to
Congress in 2009, moved to BJP and lost in 2014,
then re‑joined the NCP and won in 2019 and 2024
.
Kokate’s long career and
frequent party switches
show political
adaptability but can
cause trust issues. His
ministerial experience
gives him deep networks
in the state bureaucracy.

In 2024 he won 138,565 votes (57.36 %) against

Strong personal vote

Strengths
1. Deep local network – decades of MLA service
provide strong ties with village leaders, co‑operative
societies and administrators. 2. Government clout –
experience as cabinet minister helps deliver state
funds and projects to the constituency. 3. Personal
following – despite party switching, many voters

These strengths make
him a formidable
opponent and major
power centre in Sinnar

Weaknesses
1. Controversies – Kokate was convicted in a 1995
cheating and forgery case related to a housing
scheme for the economically weaker sections. The
sessions court upheld a two‑year sentence; the
Bombay High Court suspended the sentence but
refused to stay the conviction. Kokate resigned
as Sports and Youth Affairs Minister after the
conviction was upheld. 2. Anti‑incumbency –
after multiple terms, voters may seek change. 3. 
Party switching – frequent party changes may
reduce ideological credibility.
The conviction and
resignation have
damaged his public
image. Any by‑election or
further legal trouble
could erode support.
Digital
presence
Kokate’s social‑media activity is limited; his campaign
relies more on offline networks. Local news coverage
shows his statements and events but he lacks a
strong online brand.
Opportunity for
Uday Sangle to dominate
digital space and attract
youth and urban voters.
Implications for Uday Sangle
Highlight Kokate’s legal issues and anti‑incumbency sentiment. Stress the need for
transparent, corruption‑free leadership.
Capitalise on Kokate’s limited digital presence with a robust online campaign targeting young
voters and urban areas.
Present Sangle as a clean, stable alternative contrasting Kokate’s frequent party switches.

2 – Rajabhau Waje (Shiv Sena – Shinde faction & current MP)
Aspect Evidence & details Analysis
Background
Waje belongs to a Maratha political family. He
served as MLA for Sinnar from 2014 to 2019 and
is currently Member of Parliament from Nashik
(elected in 2024). The article notes he led
farmers’ agitations and his panel took control of
the Sinnar Agricultural Produce Market
Committee (APMC) from Kokate’s supporters in
2023 .
His farmer‑centric activism
and successful takeover of
the APMC make him a
champion of agrarian issues.
Being MP gives him
statewide visibility.
Electoral
history
Waje won the 2014 assembly election (Shiv Sena)
and lost to Kokate in 2019. He then contested the
2024 Lok Sabha election from Nashik and won
.
Waje’s shift from assembly to
parliament indicates
ambition beyond Sinnar . If he
enters state politics again, he
could split anti‑Kokate votes.
Strengths
1. Farmer connection – known for leading
farmers’ movements and addressing agrarian
distress . 2. Family network – part of a
Maratha political family with long‑standing
influence. 3. Control of APMC – his faction
gained control of Sinnar APMC, reducing Kokate’s
hold .
He appeals to agrarian and
Maratha voters. The APMC
victory shows organisational
strength and ability to wrest
local institutions from rivals.
Weaknesses
1. Split focus – as MP he must balance national
duties with local political campaigns. 2. Party
faction – belongs to the Shinde faction of
Shiv Sena; the Thackeray faction may run its own
candidate, splitting votes. 3. Previous loss – lost
the 2019 assembly election, indicating
vulnerability.
Waje’s return to Sinnar
politics could divide
opposition votes or attract
anti‑incumbent voters
depending on his strategy.
Digital
presence
Active on social media; posts updates on
parliamentary work and local events.
His online presence appeals
to youth and urban voters
but lacks hyper‑local
campaigning compared with
Uday Sangle’s potential.
Implications for Uday Sangle
Position Sangle as the authentic local alternative – emphasise his continuous focus on Sinnar
while Waje pursues national politics.
Build alliances with disgruntled Shiv Sena (Thackeray faction) and other groups to consolidate
anti‑incumbent votes.
Address agrarian issues prominently to compete with Waje’s farmer credentials.

3 – Other Potential Competitors
Shiv Sena (Thackeray faction). They may field a candidate to regain relevance after the 2019
defeat. Their vote base overlaps with Waje’s; a three‑cornered race could benefit the incumbent
if the opposition splits.
Independent / smaller parties. Local sarpanches or independent candidates can garner a few
thousand votes, particularly in villages where they have personal networks. While unlikely to win,
they can influence the margin.
Opportunities for Uday Sangle
Strong grassroots network. Although he lost in 2024, Uday Sangle secured over 97,000 votes
, demonstrating a substantial base. His support comes from youth, rural workers and those
dissatisfied with the incumbent’s style. Sangle’s wife, Sheetal Sangle, previously served as
Nashik Zilla Parishad president and supported Eknath Shinde’s faction of Shiv Sena during the
party split . This gave the family cross‑party connections and rural influence.
Anti‑corruption narrative. Kokate’s conviction for cheating in an EWS housing scheme
provides a potent narrative for a campaign centred on honesty and accountability.
Digital engagement. Both Kokate and Waje have limited digital strategies. Uday Sangle can
build an aggressive online presence – using Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp groups and local
language video content – to mobilise youth and first‑time voters. Highlighting local
development, real‑time problem solving and transparency can differentiate him from older
politicians.
Alliance building. Forging alliances with smaller parties, farmers’ groups and influential village
heads can consolidate votes. Outreach to Shiv Sena (Uddhav Thackeray faction) supporters, who
oppose the Shinde group, may expand Sangle’s base.
Issue‑based campaigning. Focus on irrigation, farm prices, local employment, road repair and
anti‑corruption. Showcase personal involvement in village disputes, youth sports programmes
and social services to strengthen his community reputation.
Conclusion
Manikrao Kokate remains the dominant figure in Sinnar politics due to his long service, resource
networks and vote bank. However , his legal troubles and anti‑incumbency sentiment offer an
opening for challengers. Rajabhau Waje’s re‑entry to state politics could split opposition votes or create
a triangular contest. To succeed, Uday Sangle must position himself as the clean, energetic alternative.
A well‑researched digital and grassroots campaign, combined with alliances and clear messaging on
local issues, can convert his existing support into a winning coalition.
